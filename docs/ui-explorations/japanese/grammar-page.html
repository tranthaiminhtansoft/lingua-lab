N5 Grammar · Minna no Nihongo

Lesson 1 はじめまして

Build your first Japanese self-introductions: identify people and things, make a negative sentence, ask a polite question, add shared information, and connect nouns.

文法Grammar / N5

Lesson outline / 目次

  1. Say what something is.
  2. Make a polite negative.
  3. Ask and answer yes/no questions.
  4. Use も for “also”.
  5. Use の to link nouns.

Quick recap / まとめ

  • は marks the topic (read wa here).
  • です makes a polite statement.
  • ではありません makes it politely negative.
  • か turns a sentence into a question.
  • も means “also / too”.
  • の shows a noun-to-noun relationship.

Five core patterns / 基本文型

1. Identify something

N1 は N2 です

N1 wa N2 desu

Use

Use this polite pattern to say that N1 is N2.

Japanese example

わたし は マリア です。

Watashi wa Maria desu.

English meaning: I am Maria.

Mini dialogue / context

A: マリア は がくせい？

A: Maria wa gakusei?

B: うん、がくせい だよ。

B: Un, gakusei da yo.

Casual: A: Is Maria a student? B: Yeah, she is.

Practice: Say “I am a student.” → わたし は がくせい です。

2. Say what something is not

N1 は N2 じゃありません

N1 wa N2 dewaja arimasen(ja is the contracted form of dewa)

Use

Use this polite negative to say that N1 is not N2.

Japanese example

わたし は せんせい ではありません。

Watashi wa sensei dewa arimasen.

English meaning: I am not a teacher.

Mini dialogue / context

A: せんせい ですか。

A: Sensei desu ka.

B: いいえ、せんせいじゃありません。

B: Iie, sensei ja arimasen.

A: Are you a teacher? B: No, I am not.

Conversation note: じゃありません is a more natural conversational form of ではありません; both are polite.

Practice: Say “Tanaka is not a doctor.” → たなかさん は いしゃ ではありません。

3. Ask a polite question

N1 は N2 ですか

N1 wa N2 desu ka

Use

Add か at the end to ask whether N1 is N2. Answer with はい (yes) or いいえ (no).

Japanese example

あなた は がくせい ですか。

Anata wa gakusei desu ka.

English meaning: Are you a student?

Mini dialogue / context

A: サントスさん、ブラジルじん？

A: Santosu-san, Burajiru-jin?

B: うん、ブラジルじん だよ。

B: Un, Burajiru-jin da yo.

Casual: A: Is Ms. Santos Brazilian? B: Yeah, she is.

Practice: Ask “Is Mr. Kim a teacher?” → キムさん は せんせい ですか。

4. Add shared information

N1 も N2 です

N1 mo N2 desu

Use

Use も instead of は when N1 is also N2.

Japanese example

ミラーさん も かいしゃいん です。

Mirā-san mo kaishain desu.

English meaning: Mr. Miller is also a company employee.

Mini dialogue / context

A: わたし は がくせい です。

A: Watashi wa gakusei desu.

B: わたし も がくせい です。

B: Watashi mo gakusei desu.

A: I am a student. B: I am a student too.

Practice: Reply “I am Japanese too.” → わたし も にほんじん です。

5. Connect two nouns

N1 の N2

N1 no N2

Use

Use の between nouns for relationships such as possession, affiliation, or description.

Japanese example

わたし は IMC の しゃいん です。

Watashi wa IMC no shain desu.

English meaning: I am an IMC employee.

Mini dialogue / context

A: かいしゃ の なまえ は？

A: Kaisha no namae wa?

B: IMC です。

B: IMC desu.

A: What is the company’s name? B: It is IMC.

Practice: Make “Japanese teacher.” → にほんご の せんせい

Register / Politeness notes / 丁寧さ

Understanding politeness levels helps you choose the appropriate expression. The labels below describe the register of each form.

Affirmative copula

です · desu
PoliteUsed in polite conversation and situations that call for respectful distance.Example: 田中さんは先生です。
Tanaka-san wa sensei desu.
English meaning: Mr. Tanaka is a teacher.

だ · da
Casual / plainCommon with friends and family or in informal writing; avoid it when politeness is required.Example: 田中は先生だ。
Tanaka wa sensei da.
English meaning: Tanaka is a teacher.

Negative copula

ではありません · dewa arimasen
Polite / formalA polite, formal negative suitable for class, work, and official announcements.Example: 田中さんは先生ではありません。
Tanaka-san wa sensei dewa arimasen.
English meaning: Mr. Tanaka is not a teacher.

じゃありません · ja arimasen
Conversational politeStill polite, but more natural in everyday conversation.Example: 田中さんは先生じゃありません。
Tanaka-san wa sensei ja arimasen.
English meaning: Mr. Tanaka is not a teacher.

ではない · dewa nai
Plain negativeA plain negative often found in writing or neutral speech that does not use polite forms.Example: 田中は先生ではない。
Tanaka wa sensei dewa nai.
English meaning: Tanaka is not a teacher.

じゃない · ja nai
Casual negativeAn informal negative common when speaking with friends or family.Example: 田中は先生じゃない。
Tanaka wa sensei ja nai.
English meaning: Tanaka is not a teacher.

Questions

ですか · desu ka
Polite questionA polite question form made by adding か to a polite sentence.Example: 田中さんは先生ですか。
Tanaka-san wa sensei desu ka.
English meaning: Is Mr. Tanaka a teacher?

の？ · no?
Casual questionAn informal question marker in conversation; its suitability depends on context and the speakers’ relationship.Example: 何をしているの？
Nani o shite iru no?
English meaning: What are you doing?

学生？ · gakusei?
Plain question · rising intonationA plain statement can become a question through rising intonation alone, without か.Example: 学生？
Gakusei?
English meaning: Are you a student?

Particles do not set register

も · mo
Register-neutral particleも does not mark politeness by itself; the rest of the sentence, such as です or だ, determines the register.Example: わたしも学生です。
Watashi mo gakusei desu.
English meaning: I am also a student.

の · no
Register-neutral particleThe noun-linking の does not mark politeness by itself; its register depends on the surrounding sentence.Example: 日本語の先生です。
Nihongo no sensei desu.
English meaning: I am a Japanese-language teacher.

Study note: は is written ha but pronounced wa when it marks the topic. No account, network request, external asset, or tracking is used on this lesson page.
